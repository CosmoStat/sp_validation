2026 · 09 · 11·mccd-vs-psfex

With the same n(z), MCCD and PSFEx give the same S8. The 0.06 gap between the two catalogues as they stand comes entirely from their n(z).

§ State

The MCCD catalogue (SP_v1.3.6.3_leak_corr) now has converged nautilus chains beside the PSFEx fiducial (SP_v1.4.6.3_leak_corr). Both use one pipeline: blind A, 1–250′ in 20 bins, the 12–83′ cut on ξ±, τ0,2 in the likelihood, and the same sampler, template and priors. Run on its own inputs, MCCD gives S8 = 0.741 ± 0.056 against PSFEx's 0.801 ± 0.069.

Rerun with the PSFEx n(z), MCCD gives 0.816 ± 0.070, a shift of +0.015 (≤ 0.4σ). So the PSF model and the catalogue behind it barely move cosmology. The 0.06 comes from the two n(z), which have mean z 0.682 and 0.620. They come from different SOM calibrations, and each carries its own blind.

Your decision: which comparison is the honest headline. The own-n(z) comparison mixes two independent blinding offsets. The borrowed-n(z) comparison is blind-consistent, but it applies the v1.4.6.3 n(z) to a v1.3 galaxy selection. Nothing else blocks.

The shift is an n(z) effect, not a PSF-model effect

Swapping only the n(z) moves MCCD by +0.075 in S8. Swapping only the PSF-leakage prior moves it by 0.0001. A higher-z n(z) raises the lensing efficiency, so the same ξ± needs a lower S8. The 2022 n(z) has mean z 0.682, against 0.620 for v1.4.6.3, and blinds B and C sit about ±0.025 around A in both sets. Depending on which letter is true in each set, the unblinded mean-z difference lies anywhere between 0.013 and 0.11. The A-vs-A gap is therefore as much a statement about the blinds as about the catalogues. The δz prior (−0.030 ± 0.018) was derived for the v1.4.6.3 n(z) and is shared by all chains.

MCCD's smaller σ(S8) on its own n(z) (0.056 against 0.070 with the PSFEx n(z)) also comes from the n(z), through the higher signal at higher z. On the same n(z), MCCD's constraint equals PSFEx's. That holds although MCCD's ξ errors are 29–38 % larger per bin: its footprint is 29 % smaller and its neff 8 % lower.

MCCD's PSF residuals are smaller at the inference scales; the leakage prior is catalogue-specific but inert for S8

The shared prior β = 0.81 ± 0.115 in priors_psf.ini is PSFEx's own τ fit: a GLS fit gives 0.80 ± 0.105. MCCD's τ gives β = 0.185 ± 0.115. On MCCD, the shared prior and the τ data in the likelihood meet halfway (posterior 0.50). With an own-τ prior, the posterior follows MCCD (0.18). S8 does not care, because β multiplies MCCD's small ρ1 and ρ2. The same prior also counts the τ data twice, for any catalogue. Details are in the sub-fiber mccd-vs-psfex/psf-leakage-prior.

How correlated the two S8 estimates are

The v1.3 footprint lies almost entirely inside the v1.4 one. For nested areas, the cosmic-variance correlation is √(A1.3/A1.4) = 0.84. Much of the imaging is shared, so the shape noise is correlated too. The table therefore quotes shifts at ρ = 0, as a lower bound on significance, and at ρ = 0.84, the better guess. The true ρ needs matched mocks or a galaxy cross-match. estimate Either way, the borrowed-n(z) shift stays below 0.5σ.

§ Open

What is not settled.

  • Which n(z), which blind, for the MCCD headline. The 2022 SOM n(z) (Wittje & Hildebrandt, CFHTLenS W3, for shapepipe_2022_v1.0) is the one calibrated for this selection. But its blind A and v1.4.6.3's blind A are independent draws. Resolving this takes your knowledge of which letter is true in each set, or a decision to quote only the borrowed-n(z) comparison for the PSF-model question.
  • δz and m priors for v1.3. Both chains use the v1.4.6.3 δz (−0.030 ± 0.018) and m (−0.0057 ± 0.014) priors. A fresh image-sim m is the job of the sibling imsim-mbias. A δz calibration for the 2022 n(z) would need the SOM team. With the δz prior at 0.018 against a 0.062 mean-z difference, this caveat does not change the verdict.
  • PSFEx's legacy npsf = 0.752. The in-footprint value would be 0.551, which means about 1.37× more PSF noise in its τ covariance, so α and β would be less tightly constrained. This touches PSFEx α/β, and S8 only through β. It would need a PSFEx τ covariance recompute and a new PSFEx chain (~3–9 h), which the spec leaves out unless you want strict like-for-like.
  • CosmoCov n(z) convention. CosmoCov's multihisto reader takes the first n(z) column as lower bin edges, while both n(z) files are on centres. Both covariances therefore use an n(z) shifted up by half a bin (0.0125), consistently. It is the same for both runs; I estimate a few per cent in amplitude but have not measured it. estimate
